# Public source options for the assessment

**Sources checked on 30 October 2026.** This optional guide identifies public material relevant to cargo hold preparation, cleanliness and inspection. It does not prescribe a collection, change the [assessment requirements](INSTRUCTIONS.md), or provide evaluation questions or answers. Select and justify your own sources. For the 3–4-hour exercise, inspect one promising option first; reading the whole list is unnecessary.

The strongest starting points found are the three official documents below, published by **USDA** and Australia's **Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF)**. They offer relevant text and a published basis for reuse. Some shorter maritime insurance guides are easy to access but have restrictive or unclear reuse conditions; those are distinguished below.

## 1. What was checked

The linked content was opened through web browsing tools without an account or payment. The review checked substantive content, English language, extractable text, document length/version where available, and the publisher's available copyright or terms pages. PDF text was readable without OCR; tables still require care during extraction.

These are access and suitability observations as of the check date. They do not establish operational currency in every jurisdiction or permission for every possible use. No full corpus has been downloaded, packaged or supplied with this guide, and no model ingestion has been tested. The selection suggestions below are editorial recommendations.

### A1 — Practical considerations

This is US grain/commodity inspection guidance, not a universal cleanliness standard for every cargo or port. Its age should be recorded explicitly. The [official FGIS directive index](https://www.ams.usda.gov/about-ams/fgis-program-directives) lists the same directive and date; this verifies its listing, not that every statement is independently current. Prefer the vessel-relevant material over unrelated land-carrier procedures.

The [USDA copyright policy](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/policies-and-links), under “Digital Rights and Copyright”, says most website information is public domain and may be copied or distributed with appropriate credit, while identifying exceptions for protected material. The checked directive is an official agency publication; no separate copyright restriction was found in its extracted text. That supports selecting its official text, subject to checking any separately credited content. Attribute USDA and avoid using agency branding as endorsement.

### A2 and A3 — Practical considerations

These are distinct documents from **one publisher**: policy/process guidance and a detailed work instruction. Either may be used on its own; the assessment does not require multiple publishers. Their Australian export context must remain visible when comparing them with another jurisdiction's guidance.

The files refer to departmental systems and authorised officers. You only need the public text; access to those systems or an inspector account is unnecessary. The prototype should explain the selected documents, not execute an official inspection or certification workflow. Use the scope definitions and preserve the meaning of conditional table rows.

DAFF's [copyright notice](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/about/copyright) permits reuse under **CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise stated**, excluding the department logo, Commonwealth Coat of Arms and material with separate third-party terms. No separate copyright notice was found in the extracted text of these two PDFs. Prefer their text, retain attribution and a link to the licence, and identify your extraction or formatting changes. The documents' controlled-version notices make recording the exact downloaded edition particularly useful.

## 5. Keep source selection within the time box

Start with one of A1–A3 if you want material with a clearer reuse basis. These are alternatives, not a set to ingest. Spend about **15–20 minutes** selecting short, coherent excerpts from **1–2 documents**. Aim for roughly **2–6 pages or 1,000–3,000 words in total**; this is a sizing guide, not a quota. The table's page ranges are places to inspect, not required selections. Preserve necessary context and use the smaller scope if it supports the two cases.

These sources support a focus on **hold cleanliness and inspection** within the task. They do not collectively establish every cleaning method for every cargo, and differences between jurisdictions should not be silently merged into a universal rule.

For the material you actually choose:

1. Download or open the official source yourself and confirm its edition and access conditions. Search snippets can describe older versions: the DAFF files opened here identify July 2026 editions.
2. Select coherent sections and record what you used. **One publisher is enough**; full manuals and a comparison across jurisdictions are unnecessary.
3. Check a short text extraction against the original, particularly table conditions, numbering and footnotes. Keep document IDs and original page/section references.
4. Record the original URL, title, publisher, edition/date if shown, access date, selected sections and applicable reuse basis in the README. A content hash is optional. Note missing publication dates rather than inferring them from a URL or copyright footer.
5. Share permitted text with attribution, or provide acquisition instructions as appropriate. A download script or the choice of model does not override restrictions on the underlying material's use.

No particular publisher, source combination, ingestion method or model provider is required. Follow the task's local application and remote model API constraints. The candidate remains responsible for source selection, evaluation and the implemented system.
